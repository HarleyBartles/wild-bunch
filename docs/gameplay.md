# Current gameplay rules

The [feature matrix](features.md) owns capability status, dependencies and deferred features. This page explains the retained local game's rules; it does not claim that every path has runtime or deployment qualification. [Decision records](decisions/README.md) preserve durable decisions and their history.

## Starting and resuming

Setup requires a character name. Difficulty defaults to Standard (Easy, Standard, Challenging and Brutal); randomness defaults to Classic (Boring, Classic, Adventurous and Wild). Each fresh setup visit offers a fresh editable UUID, retained while editing that draft. Invalid names, UUIDs, enum values or contradictory inputs fail rather than selecting replacements. Difficulty controls pressure and randomness controls variation; neither rerolls established mystery facts.

Go settles the world, seven-member gang, identities, culprit and case. The prologue reads that saved case and its opening lead; acknowledgement only advances phase. The same world map supports explicit first-town selection and later travel. First selection and free arrival commit together, without journey time, duplicate grants or a second confirmation. Subsequent selection commands, including the same town, are illegal. Transport recovery identifies the original operation and canonical request, rather than treating matching town arguments as another lawful choice. Reusing an operation ID with changed input conflicts. New setup cannot displace an active game.

Resume restores the recorded phase. Browser IDs, routes and cached resources are presentation state, not authority. After an uncertain response, reconcile with server state and retain the original startup request for its supported retry. Start Over cancellation changes nothing; confirmation durably archives before returning to setup. Archive remains committed without creating a replacement. History remains readable, further gameplay is rejected, and retry cannot append a second archive. Account-backed ownership and discovery remain future work.

## Towns, stores and resources

Every town has Saloon, Sheriff, Store and Telegraph buildings. Layouts are generated with the world and persist across visits, reload and replay. Building presence and action usability are separate: Telegraph is disabled. Store stock and prices vary with prosperity, not vendor identity. One offered item has one price per tier. Horse feed costs $1 in Boomtown/Prosperous, $1.25 in Poor and $1.50 in Destitute; consolidation preserves the other supported stock and prices. Purchase identifies the town, item and quantity, with server validation of the current offered catalog.

Equipment is unique per type, consumables stack, and invalid quantities, unaffordable purchases or duplicate equipment cannot charge the wallet. The retained firearm is the revolver with its compatible ammunition. Horse death removes the horse and retains its saddle; buying a later horse is allowed. Arrival refills an owned canteen and never grants an unowned one. Cash, bounties, settlement amounts, item quantities and usable charges are nonnegative; zero is valid. Gameplay losses record what was actually removed, including progressive losses within one day.

At daily trail upkeep, available food feeds that day and consumes one unit. Consuming the last meal does not also cause starvation. An unfed day costs 25 HP once; losing food later affects the next meal. Refresh, preview, pending encounter choices, retries and replay cannot charge another day. Damage floors at zero. Any implemented cause reaching zero commits terminal death with the final health in the same operation; no later encounter, reward or arrival success follows that lethal operation. Dead games remain readable and allow confirmed Start Over, while rejecting further gameplay.

## Investigation and settlement

The opening lead has Prologue provenance. Sheriff Office poster reads reveal one clue at a time, and retained saloon gossip and observation disclose their actual facts. There is no town noticeboard, private sheriff-records interaction or active Telegraph clue feed. Public warrant terms may disclose gang affiliation; hidden culprit flags, release counters and internal routing classifications are not player knowledge.

Saloon observation shows distinguishing features without confirming identity. A take-in requires a declared name and validation against the actual present person. A blank declaration cannot consume that person. Citizens cooperate even without a gun, are released and incur a $10 fine capped at available cash, with no debt, bounty or roundup credit. Wanted people require gun ownership, including an unloaded gun; ammunition alone is insufficient. Without a gun they refuse, without invented flight, injury or capture. With a gun, correct naming permits the recorded bounty settlement; wrong naming causes release and the capped fine, without bounty or roundup credit. Server events establish these outcomes, not browser-supplied identity or results.

Five distinct lawful settlements of the six non-culprit members of the seven-member gang release the established culprit into encounter eligibility. Four do not. Duplicate settlements, clue reads, citizens, wrong names, refusal, unrelated bounties and the culprit's own settlement do not advance the gate. Release changes availability, never identity, and does not guarantee an immediate encounter or exclude remaining members. Public posters may identify the culprit before release. No hunt-winning or culprit-killing flow is claimed; their future rules remain in the matrix.

## Casebook and journal

The casebook retains learned notes, clues, copied warrants and their explicit associations after capture, alongside recorded brought-to-justice status. Same-name people remain distinct. An unnamed report is not assigned to a suspect through hidden links, matching features or prose. Preserve authored claims, actual source, stable identity where learned, and recorded time/place/direction. Unknown facts remain unknown and phase-inapplicable views remain absent. The player makes deductions; the casebook does not turn matching features into a named sighting, a resolved lead or culprit confirmation.

Diary, notebook and journal are one semantic player history. Application-owned narration translates established events into in-world language, preserving recorded authored text. Full and journey views select the same occurrences by recorded identity and facts, never prose matching or current location. They report actual purchases, investigations, fines, settlements, travel effects, arrival and death without duplicating one occurrence or inventing causes, ammo expenditure, vendor visits or timestamps. Developer diagnostics are a separate audience of the same authoritative history. Reads, cache repairs and hidden generation facts do not automatically become player journal entries.

## State, replay and history

GameSession is the command consistency boundary; cohesive children own local rules and state without sibling mutation or independent persistence. Commands decide and record typed facts. Applying events uses recorded facts without RNG, current clocks or mutable content replacing history. Query ports return audience-safe derived views rather than loading a command aggregate to execute gameplay.

Events and current snapshots/projections commit in one unit of work with expected-position concurrency. Loads use one coherent stream position. Cache validity covers position, schema/projection version, completeness and supported invariants, not merely parseable JSON or matching version integers. Healthy caches serve normal requests. Invalid caches rebuild once from supported ordered/upcasted history and return through ordinary ports; repair cannot overwrite a newer commit. Query recovery does not emit gameplay events, resample salts or advance time. Invalid authoritative history fails explicitly rather than inventing defaults or replacing the game. Legitimate optional absence remains absent. CaseFile discovered membership starts with the generated discovery list and adds only roster-linked newly revealed clues in the generated public pool; it is an ordinal ID set, not inferred knowledge.

The one-time pre-alpha transition discards disposable playthroughs and dependent events/caches through a forward migration while preserving schema history and unrelated data. This is not a recurring reset policy. Supported future history changes need explicit migration, upcasting or deliberately scoped invalidation. There is no obligation to preserve retired gameplay codecs solely for discarded playtests, and no permission to fabricate missing historical facts.

# Town Prosperity Bible

This document defines the shared prosperity ladder for town-hub buildings.
It owns tier language for every building family and stays separate from the
identity bibles.

## Prosperity ladder

- `destitute`: roughest and most worn end of the ladder.
- `poor`: midpoint bridge between destitute and prosperous; repaired and
  serviceable, but plainly aged, lightly ornamented, and visibly below the
  polished middle tier.
- `prosperous`: maintained, polished, and clearly better kept than poor, but
  not yet the richest expression.
- `boomtown`: richest and most ornate end of the ladder.

`poor` is not a new family, camera, or footprint contract. It is the same
building family rendered at a middle-low maintenance level.

## Shared tier rules

- The tier change should come from finish, maintenance, ornament, signage, and
  completeness.
- The tier change should not come from camera changes, footprint changes, or a
  different silhouette.
- Every building family uses the same prosperity ladder unless a family bible
  explicitly says it is an exception.
- Background buildings use the same tier ladder as the named building families.
- `destitute` should read rough and under-maintained, `poor` repaired and
  serviceable, `prosperous` polished and established, and `boomtown` richest
  and most ornate.
- Let the tier progression show up in roof condition, trim, porch finish,
  signage, and facade completeness.
- `destitute` can show patchwork boards, worn roof material, and missing trim.
- `poor` can show repaired wood, faded paint, and just enough completeness to
  feel serviceable.
- `prosperous` can show cleaner boards, stronger trim, and more deliberate
  ornament.
- `boomtown` can show the fullest trim, the richest facade detail, and the most
  complete frontage the family can hold.
- If these tier rules look stale or contradictory during a current task, fix
  them as part of that task instead of deferring the update.

## Tier guardrails

- Do: Make the tier read visible through repair level, paint loss, trim, and
  ornament.
- Do not: Do not make prosperity depend on a different building shape or a new
  view set.

- Do: Keep the tier language stable across families.
- Do not: Do not invent family-specific tier names for the shared ladder.
